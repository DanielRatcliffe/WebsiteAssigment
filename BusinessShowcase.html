Web development

Brick Oven Pizza: digital transformation

My role
Student developer for the restaurant prototype, taking the role of Chief Technology Officer within the assignment's fictional business scenario.
The problem
Brick Oven Pizza needs to improve its online presence and make information easier for customers to access. Relying on printed menus and telephone enquiries limits how easily customers can explore prices, discover offers and request a table.
My solution
I developed a restaurant demonstration within my portfolio using HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It includes an online menu, a promotional offer and a booking request form. The form checks the customer's details and prepares an email containing their request.
Outcome
The prototype demonstrates a customer journey from browsing the menu to preparing a booking request. Providing clear information online could reduce routine enquiries and support customer engagement. These are expected benefits: the restaurant is fictional, and no business results have been measured. The prototype does not check live availability or confirm reservations.
Skills and approaches used
HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, responsive layouts, form validation, accessible labels, business requirements analysis and GitHub version control.

Development rationale

  • One page with section links: keeping the restaurant demonstration on the Problem-Solving page meets the five-page limit. Its navigation links move visitors directly to the relevant sections.
  • A clear customer journey: the menu and offer appear before the booking form, allowing visitors to explore their options before deciding whether to request a table.
  • A separate restaurant identity: different colours and fonts distinguish the demonstration from the portfolio. Restaurant styles are scoped to its own container so they do not change the appearance of the other pages.
  • Accessible, flexible presentation: labelled form controls, descriptive image text, visible keyboard focus and flexible layouts support visitors using different devices and ways of navigating.
  • Email-based booking requests: browser validation and JavaScript check details before preparing an email. A text preview also allows the message to be copied if an email application is unavailable. This demonstrates the process without requiring a live database.

Database design

Relational database assignment

My role
I completed a university assignment involving the design of a relational database. My responsibility was to interpret the supplied requirements and translate them into an organised structure of tables and relationships. This required both technical knowledge and careful analysis of how different pieces of information connect
The problem
The requirements described information that needed to be stored and linked together. The challenge was to decide what belonged in each table and how the tables should relate to one another. I also needed to consider unnecessary duplication and the importance of keeping related information consistent throughout the design.
My solution
I used entity-relationship diagrams to represent the structure visually and show the connections between entities. I applied normalisation principles to organise the information and reduce unnecessary repetition. I also used SQL to support the design of tables and their relationships. Together, these approaches helped me connect the written requirements with a technical design and consider how the different parts would work together.
Outcome
I produced a relational database design and strengthened my ability to analyse requirements and solve technical problems logically. The assignment helped me understand why planning the structure of information is an important part of developing a useful business system.
Skills and approaches used
SQL, entity-relationship diagrams, normalisation, requirements analysis and logical problem-solving. The project developed my ability to break a larger technical task into smaller design decisions and consider how each decision affects the overall structure.

Business planning and teamwork

AI drone business proposal

My role
I worked as a university group member on a business proposal for a fictional AI-powered drone company. When participation within the team became uneven, I took on additional organisational responsibilities, helping coordinate communication and redistribute outstanding tasks so the group could continue developing the proposal.
The problem
The project required us to consider financial planning, cybersecurity and operational risks alongside the proposed use of AI-powered drones. These areas needed to form a coherent business proposal. We also had to coordinate contributions across the group, which became more difficult when participation changed and some tasks needed to be reassigned.
My solution
I helped the group communicate about the remaining work and redistribute tasks. This meant adapting my involvement to the team's needs and supporting a shared understanding of what still needed to be completed. The experience required flexibility and a practical focus on moving the assignment forward. It also showed me how communication and task coordination can affect the progress of a project involving several contributors.
Outcome
I contributed towards a completed business proposal and group presentation. The project strengthened my teamwork, adaptability and organisational skills. Considering financial, cybersecurity and operational issues also helped me understand how business decisions influence the development of a technology-based idea.
Skills and approaches used
Teamwork, communication, task coordination, adaptability and business planning, supported by awareness of financial, cybersecurity and operational risks. A key lesson was the importance of clear responsibilities within a team. In future group projects, I would aim to agree task ownership earlier and use regular progress checks to identify unfinished work sooner.
